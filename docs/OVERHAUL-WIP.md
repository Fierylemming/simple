# Overhaul in progress — not a release

The user requested a single completed release. This branch is a work in progress, not a distribution-ready mod or jar recommendation. Do not treat compilation as proof of in-game correctness. Back up worlds and use a disposable test world if testing the branch.

## Decisions

- `/time` catches up daily food and other daily effects, but does not fast-forward walking, gathering or building. Large skips can starve citizens.
- A new settlement begins with a town hall, food, a farm and four workstation blocks; there are no initial beds. Villagers should shelter in the hall or houses at night.
- Three level-one houses per culture; expansion should respond to needs and affordable materials rather than a fixed day timer.
- User requested a single completed release, including accurate physical work for every profession and social behaviours.

## Work started

Expansion now checks on normal colony ticks, reports blockers and does not stall on the previously unsupported next-building type. It queues an affordable house if shelter capacity is low, including overcrowding at the population cap. Housing searches loaded plots on a dense grid, considers the cost of each house variant, and requires a clear entrance; it does not spend stock merely to plan. Construction can craft a small set of oak components and has preliminary movement, with an occupied-placement check. Legacy unsupported blueprints no longer block house expansion. Only explicitly designated loaded containers are scanned; unmarked player containers are excluded. Existing colony virtual stock is unchanged, but previously auto-scanned chests must be opted in again with a Building Tool (sneak-use each chest/barrel/shulker half, requiring leader status or 20 reputation). Claims persist on the block entity at its original position; replacing or moving the container requires a fresh claim. Cached stock is refreshed against loaded inventories before consumption. Current v10 houses retain the v6 three-footprint plans (including an L shape) and distinct culture-dependent walls and roof silhouettes, using oak and cobblestone; older versions remain available on reload. These designs and their in-game accessibility are not yet verified. New settlements attempt to use the starter structure as a bedless town hall; a preliminary nighttime shelter navigation behaviour and crop harvesting/replanting using actual drops were added. Farmers work within five horizontal blocks of a completed designated farm near their claimed composter and craft bread from harvested wheat; uncooked wheat is no longer counted as a meal. Woodcutters now approach and cut one block at a time for short recognizable trees, recheck safety between cuts, and use stock saplings rather than creating them. Workstation-based crafting jobs now approach the claimed POI; a basic stock-backed baker has been added. The butcher no longer fabricates meat: it attacks excess unnamed adult cows or pigs near a completed butcher hut, leaves a pair, and collects only actual unmodified livestock drops from its own attack when they spawn within reach. It feeds eligible breeding pairs with real stock and relies on vanilla animal AI to produce offspring, subject to a small herd cap. These are not yet verified in a running game.

## Starter and forester follow-up

The `/colony spawn` command now looks for safe loaded plots for both a town hall and a farm before creating a colony; it reports failure rather than silently creating one without the hall. The hall can be away from the exact command position. `/colony repair <id>` attempts a missing hall only at a safe original center and reports if it cannot; it does not remove existing buildings. New colonies start with one stone pickaxe and stone axe in virtual stock. The miner can withdraw a pickaxe from colony stock, using its durability; this is not yet a physical trip to collect it. A smith can make replacements from real materials. Existing colonies do not automatically receive new tools.

The forester's conservative short, straight-trunk recognition now also recognizes several other overworld log/leaf species and uses the matching stocked sapling where a single sapling can grow. This does **not** yet meet the requirement to clear all tree types: tall, branched, multi-trunk and many natural tree forms remain unsupported; dark oak cannot be replanted as a single sapling. Species does not prove that a log was naturally generated: player property near worksites remains at risk. No new jar was uploaded by that failed run.

## Bounded crooked-tree support

Foresters can recognize certain small crooked trees with vertical logs for oak, spruce, birch, jungle, acacia and cherry. They require a single rooted base, connected logs within a strict envelope and a matching natural canopy, with every cut reachable using existing terrain. Saved log positions are validated on reload; the whole envelope is rechecked before each actual axe cut, and a real sapling is needed to replant. ## Shepherd and storage follow-up

A Shepherd can be assigned from the villager menu. Place a Building Marker and designate **Pasture**, put a loom within 12 blocks, and explicitly share a chest, barrel or shulker box. The shepherd claims the loom, walks to shared storage to collect real shears, and shears eligible unnamed adult sheep in a six-block horizontal, three-block vertical area around the still-present pasture marker. Sheep regrow wool through vanilla behaviour. A shepherd may also collect plain wheat from shared storage, carry enough for both eligible adults, and feed them in reach while leaving a two-wheat reserve; vanilla animal AI must produce the lamb. Named sheep and lambs count toward the four-sheep flock cap. Wool comes from the sheep's actual shearing drops, is carried in persisted villager cargo, and is delivered only at a reachable shared container. Full or removed storage leaves cargo with the worker; death or a profession change drops it. The sheep and tool are not created by the job. Named sheep are ignored, but unnamed player sheep near the marker are indistinguishable from intended livestock: the marker is permission to work that area, not proof of individual ownership.

A smith with a shepherd in the colony can make one spare pair of shears from two real iron ingots if shared storage has room. Like the existing smith recipes, its inputs and output currently use the provisional remote stock network; only the shepherd's tool pickup and wool delivery are physical. Generic stock deposits and couriers now respect shulker-box nesting restrictions. [CI run 43464987599](https://github.com/Fierylemming/simple/actions/runs/43464987599) passed the build and full GameTest suite at `eadfb1f`, including pasture assignment, real sheep/shears/cargo, rejected nested shulkers and the smith recipe. A completed generated level-one smithy can now be upgraded by the colony leader through the Buildings tab. A builder must be assigned, the original smithing table must still exist, and there must be an empty, loaded, flat five-by-five plot immediately east or west, clear of other recorded buildings, with sufficient shared stock including a spare smithing table. The builder constructs a separate matching-timber workshop wing with a second actual smithing table and adjacent furnace. Materials are checked without spending them when queued and taken as blocks are placed; the level changes only after completion. The plan and completed prefix persist across reload. Operators can cancel it with `/colony cancel_building <id> <original smithy position>`; the parent record and any already placed blocks remain, and spent materials are not refunded. The UI list scrolls and menu button targets are frozen when opened, so a changed list cannot redirect an upgrade to a different building. Reopen the menu to see updated data.

The fixture does not replace in-game validation. This is only a level-two smithy wing: other generated buildings and higher levels still lack physical upgrades, and the marker-house upgrade remains capacity-only. Workers still access most shared stock remotely.
